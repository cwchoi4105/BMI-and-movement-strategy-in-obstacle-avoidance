# BMI-and-Movement-Strategy-in-Obstacle-Avoidance

Increased body mass index (BMI) is strongly correlated with postural instability. Yet, we have limited information on how BMI affects sensorimotor performance and the ability to control posture in adapting to new scenarios. The Virtual Obstacle Avoidance Program (VOAP) was designed to measure participants' responses in a seated position as they navigated virtual obstacles, capturing specific postural strategies. This study explored (1) whether the VOAP is associated with postural control and if its results are consistent, and (2) the effects of BMI on VOAP parameters. Twenty-six participants (Female=15; mean age=27.2 years old±7.7; BMI range=18.7—57.9 kg/m2) completed Functional Gait Assessment (FGA) alongside VOAP, which measured leg movement strategies (Strategy, i.e., either hip adduction or abduction), Total Leg Movement (TLM), and Maximal Distance from Midline (MDM) while avoiding virtual obstacles in seated positions. FGA scores were significantly correlated with TLM (r= -0.390, p<0.001), MDM (r= -0.165, p<0.001), and Strategy (r= 0.131, p<0.001). Interclass correlation coefficients (ICCs) indicated a strong agreement among Strategy (ICCs = 0.989, p<0.001), MDM (ICCs = 0.805, p<0.001), and TLM (ICCs = 0.882, p<0.001). Higher BMI was associated with more hip adduction during the VOAP (OR=0.767, p=0.040). TLM was longer BMI (β=3.749, p=0.020) and age (β=4.099, p=0.023) increased. TLM was greatest when the obstacle appeared after movement onset (Conditions 3 and 4, both p < 0.001). The VOAP framework may effectively identify BMI-related postural differences, offering a potential basis for tailored rehabilitative assessments and interventions for enhancing postural control in individuals with obesity.
